From Static Scaling to Test-Time Compute and Verifiable Agentic Systems

November 14, 2026 · Research Note

Over the past two years, the center of gravity in machine learning has shifted from pure pre-training parameter scaling toward inference-time reasoning, process-supervised reinforcement learning, and multi-agent orchestration. Rather than treating foundation models as single-pass next-token predictors, modern architectures allocate dynamic compute budgets at test time—allowing models to search, self-verify, and backtrack across structured reasoning trajectories before committing to an output.

In my recent work across natural language processing, computer vision, and agentic AI, three open research challenges stand out as critical for real-world deployment:

  • Verifiable Reward Signals Beyond Math and Code: Extending reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) to open-ended multimodal reasoning, grounded tool use, and scientific synthesis without falling into reward hacking.
  • Grounded Multimodal Perception: Moving beyond late-fusion vision-language adapters toward native interleaved spatial-temporal representations that preserve fine-grained visual grounding during long-horizon planning.
  • Coordinated Multi-Agent Protocols: Designing lightweight communication and consensus mechanisms where specialized agents decompose complex tasks with bounded latency and formal interpretability.

Future posts here will unpack experimental findings, evaluation benchmarks, and open-source implementations exploring these directions.
